## Conclusion

The slice is safe and the machinery is proven (unit tests + the one kept
collapse + the judge transaction), but single-segment jog collapse is nearly
empty on real boards: a short jog almost always exists because the straight
line on the original layer is genuinely blocked — the dip to the other layer
is load-bearing. This confirms the baseline note's prediction: a cleanup pass
alone cannot move a 1.667x median. Next levers, in order:

1. Classify the refusals (segment_state owners): if the blockers are mostly
   other-net *vias*, a paired move (collapse + via shift) could unlock some;
   if they are tracks, local transforms are done and the lever is search-side.
2. Dogleg middles: jogs whose middle is two segments (the pattern generalises;
   the replacement becomes a checked dogleg on L instead of one segment).
3. Search-side via economics: via cost / layer preference during routing and
   in the router's own clean-up, measured against the Phase 4A baseline.
